The experiment

A fresh repository, one real suite, repeated 30 times. Can a measured timeout avoid the wasted restart when a caller guesses too low?

pallets/flask is a popular Python web framework (75,056 GitHub stars and last pushed 2026-10-30, checked 2026-11-07). It was not in the previous ten-repository benchmark. Its pytest suite completed with 494 passing tests.

Naive runs the suite with a guessed timeout G, doubling it and restarting from zero on each timeout (at most four attempts). hh runs run_tests against the same checkout: one cold record pass, followed by 29 measured calls sharing the store. Both arms use pytest and disable its cache provider.

What hh measured, and what it chose

On the cold pass hh reported basis: record, no predicted wall time and recorded 494 tests. From call 2, the budget was measured with 100% coverage. Its first warm estimates were p50 1.031 s and p90 1.050 s; on call 30 they were 1.108 s and 1.128 s. The minimum budget floor set the warm budget to 3.00 s throughout.

These p50/p90 values estimate the sum of test durations, not the whole process. hh adds startup allowance to its execution budget. The cold default 30 s budget is not a measurement. wall_s excludes some tool work; process_wall_s is the external elapsed time, used for the naive comparison.

How to read this honestly
  • hh does not make tests faster. The two unbounded plain runs had a 1.575 s median process wall; the hh warm process median was 1.888 s, about 0.313 s more. This is descriptive, not a controlled estimate of overhead.
  • The first call is the price of measuring. Its hh wall was 1.644 s (1.724 s process wall), with no prediction. The comparison above excludes this one-time record pass.
  • Small sample for the sweep. Each naive guess was tried once, after the 30 hh calls; the arms are not paired or randomized. Only G=1 s timed out. These numbers can shift with machine load, caching and dependencies.
  • Scope. One checkout, one machine and one supported runner do not establish performance across repositories. No hh timeout occurred, so resume behavior was not exercised. The 30 calls share a progressively updated store and are not independent cold trials. A plain suite check and two exploratory hh calls preceded the formal run, so filesystem and interpreter caches may already have been warm; the formal store was cleared.
  • Same suite, different invocation. Naive uses pytest tests; hh discovers and executes node IDs with its ledger plugin. All reference runs passed and every hh ledger reports 494 passed, but instrumentation and invocation differ.
